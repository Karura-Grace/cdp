# J5b new survey items: DRAFT wording for the owner to approve

Status: DRAFT, 25 October 2026. Nothing here is built, and nothing touches the instrument, the database or any seed. The wording is original and was written for the CDP. It does not use any wording, scale or model from the partner documents (ideas and structure only, owner rule).

## Decisions behind this draft
- Q-J5-12: leadership style acceptance and identification with the organisation get new survey items; motivators and demotivators get a new ranked question. Trust and security and ease of change use existing scores and need no new items.
- Q-J5-13: Claude drafts, the owner approves. The items go in instrument 1.2, used by NEW assessments only. Assessments already created stay on instrument 1.1 and their frozen scores never change.

## How they would sit in the survey
A short extra block of 10 questions at the end, after the six scenario dimensions and before the demographics. About 3 minutes. The same five point scale as the rest of the survey where a scale is used. Every item is optional to skip. Answers are stored the same way as other survey answers (no link to a person after submit) and only appear in groups of five or more.

## Block A: how you see the way leaders lead here (leadership style acceptance)
Scale for A1 to A4: Strongly disagree, Disagree, Neither, Agree, Strongly agree.
- A1. The way leaders here make decisions is a way I can work with.
- A2. When leaders here disagree with me, I understand why.
- A3. Leaders here behave in the way they ask the rest of us to behave.
- A4. I would be comfortable if a leader's style here became the norm across the whole organisation.
Reading: the platform reports the share who agree, by group, and the gap between executives and staff. It is never shown for one leader and never used to assess a person.

## Block B: how you feel about this organisation (identification)
Same scale.
- B1. When someone praises this organisation, it feels like praise for me.
- B2. I talk about this organisation as "we", not "they".
- B3. What this organisation stands for matches what I care about at work.
- B4. If I were offered a similar job elsewhere, I would need a strong reason to leave.
Reading: the share who agree, by group, shown next to the culture fit scores.

## Block C: what drives you at work (ranked)
- C1. Rank these from "drives me most" to "drives me least". Drag to reorder. All eight must be ranked.
  1. Doing work I find meaningful
  2. Being trusted to decide for myself
  3. Learning and growing
  4. Being paid fairly
  5. Working with people I like
  6. Seeing the organisation succeed
  7. Having security in my job
  8. Being recognised for what I contribute
- C2. Which two of these most take the energy out of your work here? Choose two.
  1. Unclear decisions or direction
  2. Too many rules and approvals
  3. Not being listened to
  4. Unfair treatment
  5. Too little support or resources
  6. Change that is announced but not explained
  7. Not seeing results from what we say we will do
  8. Poor cooperation between teams
Reading: the average rank of each driver and the top two energy drains, by group, with floor checks. Shown in the report as motivators and demotivators.
