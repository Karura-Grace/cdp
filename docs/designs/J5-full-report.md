# J5 full organisation report: design for the owner to review

Status: DRAFT for review, 25 October 2026. Built from the owner's answers Q-J5-1 to Q-J5-13 in the decisions log. Nothing in this document changes the survey, the database or the instrument. The new survey items are in a separate draft (J5b) and are not built.

## What was decided
- Style: Direction D (friendly symbols and rounded cards from C2, softened with the white paper, hairlines and quiet colour of clean and minimal). The culture fingerprint is drawn as quarter discs, with three views: today, preferred and the two overlaid so the gap shows.
- Length: 30 to 40 pages.
- Release: the assigned PAC Consultant releases, as today. No second approver.
- Added by the owner: more recommendations per finding, a "what to say to staff" box, the client logo beside the PAC logo, orientation symbols as page markers, section dividers with a symbol, and a running footer with confidentiality.

## What exists today (the base we extend)
The full report already exists as a PDF (`pdf-full.ts`) built from the frozen score, the stored findings and plan, and floor checked group results (`full-model.ts`). It has a cover, report details, contents, an executive summary and 13 numbered chapters (method, current culture, gaps, executives and staff, departments, employee voice, readiness, risk, hits and misses, leadership 360 summary, roadmap, KPIs), a glossary and a technical appendix, with the consultant's note under each chapter. It is plain, in a dark cover and serif body style, and it has no culture map, no fingerprint and no infographic pages.

## The playbook page, one per priority
Each priority gets one page, in this order:
1. Heading with the orientation symbol, the reading (more or less of it), the gap, the urgency and the owner role.
2. Why it matters, in plain words, from the playbook.
3. How, as short steps.
4. What to do, as **two or three recommendations per finding**, with the playbook's preferred one marked. Each is an action the plan can carry.
5. The visible signal within 30 days, what it unlocks, and what to re measure at the next pulse.
6. **What to say to staff**: a short suggested message a leader can share with their team about this priority, in plain, warm words. Drafted by the platform from the playbook, edited by the consultant before release.
7. A pre condition note when the priority is held by an open gate.

## The culture fingerprint and the map
- Fingerprint: four quarter discs, one per orientation, sized by how much of that orientation there is (50 is a full square). Today is solid, preferred is a dashed outline. The overlay lays the preferred outline on today's discs and shades the gap: orange where the organisation wants more, grey where it wants less.
- Map: the four squares with now and preferred as points, and the route between them along the axes only, passing through the stepping stone the engine names (already built in the app map). The overlaid fingerprint sits behind the points.
- Both come from the frozen score. Nothing is computed in the report layer.

## Branding
- Cover: PAC logo beside the client logo (the uploaded tenant logo, as the executive brief already does).
- Colours: the four orientation hues from the spec, PAC orange for what is the client's to act on, ink for text, white paper.
- Footer on every page: confidentiality line, report version and hash, page number.
- Dividers: soft panel with a large orientation symbol and the part title.

## Where the words come from
Data driven: every number, chart and verdict. Library driven: the playbook text (`playbook-data.ts`, `finding-text.ts`) and the report library. Consultant edited before release: the note under each chapter, and the "what to say to staff" boxes. The release check (`checkRelease`) stays as it is and refuses a report whose sections are not accepted.
