5.2Evidence by check

Two checks are partial hits, and both turn on the same trade: building Engage without losing the Perform strength at 29.64.

Miss
22.88
Innovate now

Finding. Innovate is last of four at 22.88, while the strategy depends on new models.

Evidence. People would raise it to 31.79, a shift of +8.91.

Consequence. New model work competes for time with targets that reward today’s delivery.

Action. Ring fence time and budget for a few pilots. Owner: executive team. Timing: first 90 days.

Hit
31.99
Engage in leadership

Finding. Leadership behaviour already matches the Engage target the strategy sets.

Evidence. Engage ranks first in the leadership profile at 31.99, ahead of Perform at 26.88.

Consequence. Leaders can model the change, provided staff see the same behaviour.

Action. Use leaders as visible sponsors of Engage work. Owner: chief executive. Timing: from launch.

Partial hit
−10.15
Perform, the shift wanted

Finding. Systems would have to stop rewarding some Perform habits people no longer want.

Evidence. Perform would fall from 29.64 to 19.49; it is the culture’s current anchor.

Consequence. Cutting too fast risks losing the delivery discipline clients rely on.

Action. Review targets and rewards before changing them. Owner: finance and HR leads. Timing: first 120 days.

Partial hit
24.05
Engage now

Finding. The consumer service model needs Engage and Perform together; only Perform is in place.

Evidence. Perform stands at 29.64, Engage at 24.05, while people want Engage at 35.39.

Consequence. Service quality depends on goodwill that the culture does not yet build.

Action. Make customer care part of team goals. Owner: operations lead. Timing: first 90 days.

So what

The strategy is not blocked by leaders, who already lead with Engage. It is held back by a culture that rewards delivery over new ideas, with Innovate at 22.88.
